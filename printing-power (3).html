What you build

Pick your path

Both paths ship as real, documented hardware designs. Neither one is "print a keychain."

PATH 01

Full custom build

Design a 3D printer from the frame up. You spec the motion system, the extruder, the electronics, the bed — and you justify every part choice, not just pick from a kit.

  • Frame & motion system (Cartesian, CoreXY, delta — your call)
  • Extruder & hotend selection or design
  • Full bill of materials with reasoning
  • Assembled and calibrated, or a build-ready design
PATH 02

Open-source mod

Take a real open-source printer and engineer a genuine upgrade. Real constraints, real tolerances, a machine that already exists that you have to design around.

  • A new hot end (higher temp, better flow, all-metal)
  • An AMS — automatic multi-material system
  • A stiffened or upgraded gantry
  • Any mod that measurably improves print quality

FAQ

Before you start

Do I need to already own a 3D printer?

No. You can design a full custom build without ever having touched one. If you're doing an open-source mod, it helps to have access to the printer you're modding, but a documented design based on real specs still counts.

Do I need to know CAD already?

No prior CAD or printer experience is required — you're expected to have it by the time you ship. Plenty of Hack Clubbers pick up Fusion 360 or FreeCAD specifically for this.

What counts as a "real" mod?

Something that measurably changes how the printer performs — a new hot end, a multi-material AMS, a stiffened gantry, a better cooling shroud. A cosmetic part swap doesn't count.

Do I have to actually build the thing, or just design it?

A design with a full bill of materials and reasoning is the minimum. If you print, assemble, or test it, that build log is what pushes your Spool payout up.

Why "Spools"?

Every design you ship earns Spools — named after what most of your payout turns back into. Exact redemption rates are still being tuned.
